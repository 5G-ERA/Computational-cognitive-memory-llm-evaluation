Experiment 1 · reframing the audit extension · 22 August 2026

Reconstruction and Replay

Renxi's definition: audit is the process of reconstruction and replay. His puzzle: if so, why does the audit say 96–100% and the recall say 30–60%? Because the extension implemented only the first half as its headline and filed the second as a side check. The gap between the two numbers is the gap between those two halves — and the information lives entirely in the second.

Data reserved splits, Terra + Qwen3, 1,440 calls each; Qwen3 repeat run for identical replay Oracle read only to score the detectors, under signed inspection records Status exploratory, post hoc

Where each number comes from

Half 1 · reconstruction Can this one trace be followed from evidence to output? 96–100% Level 1 of the extension. Grounded, consistent, schema-valid. Checked once per call, never repeated. At ceiling for both models, by construction: four-field schema, runner already rejects invalid responses. What it cannot see: a well-formed trace that would not come out the same way twice. The §15 join proved it: 330 of 470 reconstructible Qwen3 traces in B0 were wrong.
Half 2 · replay Does the pathway come out the same way again? 30–60% Protocol §14, filed as a "descriptive reproducibility check." Run the same context again — identically, or reworded — and compare the pathway to itself. No oracle needed: disagreement is a certified error. What it sees: every inconsistent error, with 100% precision. The recall — the share of errors that are inconsistent — is the number that separates the models and the conditions.

Reconstruction is necessary and saturated. Replay is where the discrimination lives. Under Renxi's definition, the recall number is the audit.

Two kinds of replay, and we have both

Identical replay repeats the same call and asks whether the pathway is stochastically stable. The Qwen3 repeat run gives it. Invariant replay rewords the same evidence and asks whether the pathway depends on content or on form. The three renderings give it. Invariant replay is the stronger test: a model can pass identical replay by being deterministic about a surface pattern.

So Qwen3's instability has two components, stochastic and semantic, and both grow under cognitive memory. That sounds like a cost. It is not, because of where the instability sits.

Both replays as error detectors — recall on two independent axes

A replay that disagrees with itself contains at least one error. Precision is 100% on both axes in every cell. Recall is what the model can see of its own mistakes.

identical replayinvariant replay (rewording)

The same doubling on both axes. Cognitive memory takes identical-replay recall from 16% to 37% and invariant-replay recall from 30% to 60%. Two independent replays, one gradient: M0 < B0 < B1. The pre-specified map alone makes Qwen3 more consistently wrong; cognitive memory makes its errors self-exposing.

Why: under B1 the instability moves from right answers to wrong ones. Citation stability among all-correct contexts rises from 32% to 49%; among contexts with an error it falls from 60% to 21%. The aggregate falls because errors got noisier, which is the detector working. Terra shows the faithful pattern throughout — 96% stable when right, 0–11% when wrong — and catches 73–100% of its own errors by replay.

What the extension becomes under this reading

The theory's ordering is audit-ready → evaluable → functionally auditable → audit-conformant, where the last rung compares the realised trace against a frozen reference κ*. An LLM has no κ* for its rationale. Replay is the substitute: across repetitions the pathway becomes its own reference. Reconstruction is the functionally auditable rung; replay is what κ* does for cognitive memory, done without one.

Cognitive memory makes the LLM's pathway replay more consistently when it is right and less consistently when it is wrong, so a larger share of its errors becomes self-exposing. That is a reconstruction-and-replay result, and it is stronger stated that way than as an audit result.
