03ruledSaturation

Saturation threshold and interpretation

Proposed rule, stated so that it does not depend on having seen any particular baseline:

Rule

A model-endpoint arm is saturated when its attainable improvement, 100 − B0, is smaller than the smallest effect the programme has claimed — 5.00 pp, the lower bound of the sealed KIMI2 primary interval. For a saturated arm a null B1−B0 is reported but is not treated as an informative test of the representation effect.

The classification is insensitive to the number. Any threshold between 2 pp and 10 pp classifies Terra identically. The choice therefore cannot be doing the work, which is the answer to the objection that it was made with the baseline in view.

Ruled, 19 August 2026

Threshold 5.00 pp: ACCEPTED. Post-signature analyst classification: RATIFIED — saturation is judged after signature, by the analyst, never during eligibility or effort selection. Use of saturation for inclusion or selection: PROHIBITED. Qwen3 retained regardless of baseline: CONFIRMED.

The prohibition is stricter than what was proposed here and is carried as a hard rule: saturation may inform how a result is read and may never influence which arm is kept or which setting is chosen.

04recordEffort

Candidate reasoning-effort settings and blinded selection algorithm

Candidates exposed by each endpoint: Terra low / medium / high; Qwen3 low / medium, with high exceeding the 180-second probe timeout. The selection algorithm is the amendment’s, unmodified:

  1. Exclude any setting that truncates or omits scientific input.
  2. Exclude any setting whose output-truncation rate exceeds the frozen operational limit.
  3. Exclude any setting whose JSON or schema-validity rate falls below the frozen operational limit.
  4. Among the survivors, select the lowest reasoning effort.
  5. If tied: lower median output tokens, then lower latency, then a frozen lexical ordering of setting names.

The outcome is forced, not chosen

low is the lowest setting either endpoint exposes. If it clears steps 1–3 the algorithm selects it and no sweep is required, because no higher setting can outrank it. On Terra’s completed 360-call development split at low: output truncation 0/360 = 0.000, invalid output 0/360 = 0.000, between-condition spread 0.000 — all at floor against stop limits of 0.10. The selection involves no discretion and therefore nothing for an unblinded selector to bias.

On Qwen3’s completed 360-call development split at low: output truncation 0/360 = 0.000, invalid output 0/360 = 0.000, spread 0.000. Steps 1–3 are cleared, so step 4 selects low and no higher setting was run or needed to be run. Reasoning effort is frozen at low on both arms.

Disclosure: for Terra the operator was not blinded, having already inspected that arm’s accuracy. The degeneracy above is what makes the selection defensible, not the operator’s blinding. For Qwen3 the selector is blinded as required.

05recordEstimand

Non-deterministic estimand wording

Proposed annex text, to replace the deterministic-call assumption:

For model m and condition c, the target of estimation is the probability of a correct response under the frozen benchmark, the frozen request configuration, and the named endpoint, taken over the model’s response distribution. The model-specific representation effect is the difference between the Cognitive-memory and Raw-longitudinal-evidence response probabilities. Provider randomness that is not exposed as a request parameter is treated as a property of the evaluated model-endpoint system rather than as a nuisance to be eliminated. Every exposed request parameter is frozen and reported. The replication estimates expected response accuracy under a frozen endpoint configuration; it does not claim that one prompt returns identical text on repeated calls.

Consequences are narrower than the wording suggests. Generation noise is variance, not bias; the memory-clustered bootstrap already propagates it. What is lost is exact numerical replication, which no Bedrock arm can offer in any configuration.

07ruledMultiplicity

Multiplicity rule

Ruled, 19 August 2026

Umbrella success claim: NONE. Multiplicity adjustment: none, for the single confirmatory hypothesis.

No umbrella claim is declared. There is one prespecified primary replication claim, in Qwen3, and therefore one confirmatory family and no alpha adjustment. Terra cannot substitute for a Qwen3 failure.

Declining the union claim is the deliberate choice: a “success in either arm” claim would require Holm or Bonferroni control and, with one arm expected to be saturated, would amount to an undeclared whichever-model-succeeds criterion. Should a broader cross-model claim be wanted later, it must be defined before any further result is seen, which after signature it will not be.

Defensible broad conclusion: Qwen3 replicated, or did not replicate, the KIMI2 effect on the unchanged benchmark, while Terra supplied a ceiling-control result.
